### Breaking

- The ChainSync client's `ConfigEnv` gained `leiosJumpAcceptedCallback`, invoked whenever this peer accepts a jump. For Leios the wiring records, for every header in the dynamo's candidate fragment that the jump carries, the same per-peer offer that header's `MsgRollForward` would have.

  Without it a jumper has no offers at all. It never sends the headers it jumps over, so a node that loses its dynamo is left with nobody to ask for the endorser blocks those headers announced. Agreeing to a jump asserts the peer would have sent the same headers, which is what makes treating them as its offers sound.

  That fragment is anchored where the ChainSync client's view of our own chain is, `k` back, so it sits on our immutable chain: a header it does not reach is one whose block we already hold immutably.

- `LeiosPeerVars` gained `maxAcceptedJumpSlot`, the newest slot this peer has accepted a jump through.

  Each jump carries the dynamo's whole candidate fragment rather than the part since the previous jump, so the wiring splits both the fragment and the jump's header state history at that slot before walking them. Each split is `O(log n)` in the underlying finger tree, against fragments that successive jumps overlap in everything but their last `csjcJumpSize` headers. Splitting the history at the same slot leaves its anchor holding the first kept header's predecessor state, which is the argument that header's `MsgRollForward` would have been given.

  A split finds nothing at that slot only if this peer accepted a jump on one chain and then a jump on an alternative chain, and the whole fragment is then walked. That needs the dynamo to have changed, since one dynamo may not roll back before the last jump it requested --- CSJ disengages it if it tries --- so the peer is either dishonest, or honest and close enough to the wall clock to have switched chains in between.

### Patch

- The Leios node-versus-environment harness serves ChainSync itself rather than through `chainSyncServerExample`, so that a peer can be told whether to announce reaching its tip (`setAwaitAtTip`, `WhetherToAwaitAtTip`).

  CSJ disengages a peer as soon as it says it has no more headers, and every peer in this harness runs out within a simulated instant, so nothing about CSJ could be tested before: no dynamo survived to the fetch logic's next tick. The flag is read in the same transaction that waits for more chain, so telling a peer already parked at its tip to announce it wakes that peer.
