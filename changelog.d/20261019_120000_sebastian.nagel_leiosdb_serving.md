### Breaking

- `lookupEbBody` returns the EB body in the bytes it arrived in, as `Maybe ByteString`, instead of its decoded tx hashes and sizes.
  Missing bodies are `Nothing` instead of an empty list.

- LeiosDB no longer tracks whether an EB is complete, and no longer tells what is written to it; the fetch logic knows that.
  `writeEbBody` and `writeTxs` return `()`.
  `CompletedEbs`, `subscribeEbNotifications`, `LeiosEbNotification` and the `ebs.missingTxCount` column are gone.
  `scanCompleteEbClosuresNotOlderThanSlot` infers completeness from the txs the LeiosDB holds instead.
  Until LeiosFetch announces its acquisitions, the ChainDB learns of acquired closures only at startup.

- The LeiosFetch server decodes the endorser block from the bytes it stores, rather than building it from its references.
  Its `BlockServed` trace gives the endorser block's size in bytes instead of its number of transactions.

- LeiosDB stores an EB's body in the bytes it arrived in, in a new `ebBodies` table.
  There is no migration: delete existing LeiosDB files.
